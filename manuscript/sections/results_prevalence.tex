\subsection{Prevalence of Epoch-Own Scaling}
The audit protocol was committed before any item was inspected \cite{repo_sequel}. Among ten widely used EEG decoding codebases, three scale each sample by its own statistics in their default or reported pipelines: TorchEEG \cite{zhang2024torcheeg} (\texttt{MeanStdNormalize} without supplied statistics computes the mean and standard deviation of the sample itself), BENDR through the DN3 \texttt{MappingDeep1010} transform (per-trial min-max scaling into $[-1,1]$), and BIOT (division by each sample's 95th percentile of absolute amplitude, a scale-only form, measured in Sec.~\ref{sec:results} under Table~\ref{tab:routes}). Two codebases use training or causal running statistics (braindecode \cite{schirrmeister2017deep}, EEG Conformer \cite{song2023conformer}), four apply a fixed constant (EEGNet reference code \cite{lawhern2018eegnet}, LaBraM \cite{jiang2024labram}, CBraMod \cite{wang2025cbramod}, EEGPT \cite{wang2024eegpt}), and one applies no amplitude scaling (MOABB pipelines \cite{jayaram2018moabb}).

In a fixed-rule sample of the 40 most recent arXiv papers reporting supervised EEG decoding, 28 (70\%) contain no sentence that determines the input scaling. Of the 12 that do, four scale each window or trial by its own statistics, three use training or participant-wide statistics, one uses the pre-stimulus interval, three apply a fixed constant, and one applies no scaling. Every item, its code, and its deciding source line or sentence are listed in the repository.
