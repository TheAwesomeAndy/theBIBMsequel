\section{Materials and Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Datasets}
\emph{SHAPE.} Trial-averaged affective ERPs from the Stress, Health, and the Psychophysiology of Emotion project \cite{shape}, recorded during passive viewing of negative, neutral, and pleasant pictures. After exclusion of one participant with a recording anomaly, 211 participants contribute three condition averages each (633 observations, three balanced classes, chance $1/3$). Each delivered epoch has 34 scalp channels at 1024~Hz, 1229 samples, and baseline correction over the first 205 samples (200~ms before onset). Following the BIBM 2026 pipeline \cite{lane2026bibm}, every fourth of the first 1024 samples is analysed, giving 256 samples at 256~Hz from $-200$ to $+800$~ms with onset at sample 51. Because the analysed pre-stimulus window is a subsample of the baseline window, \eqref{eq:bc} holds approximately rather than exactly in this cohort.

\emph{ERP CORE.} Five stimulus-locked paradigms of the public ERP CORE resource \cite{kappenman2021erpcore}, each with 40 participants: N170 (faces versus cars), P3 (rare versus frequent targets), N400 (unrelated versus related word pairs), N2pc (left versus right target), and MMN (deviants versus standards). The delivered epoched files are ICA-corrected, baseline-corrected over $-200$ to $0$~ms, sampled at 256~Hz, and span $-200$ to $+797$~ms. Trials flagged by the provided artifact detection are removed and the 28 scalp channels are kept. The response-locked ERN and LRP paradigms are not used, because activity before a response is genuine motor preparation and the pre-event interval is then not a null region.

\subsection{Scalings}
Each epoch and channel is scaled by one of the rules of Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}: per-epoch z-scoring (\texttt{epoch\_z}), z-scoring with post-stimulus statistics (\texttt{post\_z}), division by the pre-stimulus standard deviation (\texttt{pre\_sd}), and no scaling (\texttt{none}). On SHAPE a fifth rule divides each channel by one cohort-wide standard deviation (\texttt{global\_sd}). Only the two z-scorings use post-stimulus samples to transform earlier ones.

\subsection{Encoders}
The \emph{pre-stimulus mean} encoder averages each channel over the pre-stimulus window ($-164$ to $0$~ms on SHAPE, $-200$ to $0$~ms on ERP CORE), and a six-bin version keeps the temporal profile. The \emph{ERP-window} encoder is the BIBM baseline, with three window means per channel \cite{lane2026bibm}.

The \emph{ARSPI-Net reservoir} is the temporal encoding stage of BIBM 2026 \cite{lane2023arspinet,lane2024arspinet,lane2026bibm}: 256 LIF neurons driven independently by each channel, with membrane update $\mathbf{v}_t=(1-\beta)(1-\mathbf{z}_{t-1})\odot\mathbf{v}_{t-1}+W\mathbf{z}_{t-1}+W_\mathrm{in}u_t$, spikes $\mathbf{z}_t=\mathbb{1}[\mathbf{v}_t\ge\vartheta]$, threshold subtraction and rectification, $\beta=0.05$, $\vartheta=0.5$, Xavier-uniform weights drawn once with seed 42, and spectral radius $\rho=0.9$. Spike counts in six equal bins are compressed by one 64-component PCA basis shared across channels and fitted on training participants only. Two read-out windows are used. The BIBM window spans samples 10 to 69 ($-160$ to $+73$~ms). The \emph{onset-truncated} reservoir stops its simulation at stimulus onset and reads samples 9 to 50, so by construction it never receives a post-stimulus sample. Its firing rate under the BIBM configuration, 0.124 spikes per unit and step, reproduces the value reported in \cite{lane2026bibm}.

\emph{EEGNet-8,2} \cite{lawhern2018eegnet} is trained with the BIBM recipe (Adam, learning rate $10^{-3}$, 80 epochs, batch 64, dropout 0.25, train-only per-channel standardization) on the full epoch and, separately, on the pre-stimulus samples alone, with a 32-sample temporal kernel for the shorter input.

\subsection{Evaluation}
On SHAPE the BIBM protocol is kept exactly: \texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold} with five folds and seeds 42 to 46, participants as groups, train-only standardization, and a class-balanced L2 logistic readout ($C=1$). Out-of-fold class probabilities are summed over the five partitions, and 95\% confidence intervals come from 4000 bootstrap resamples of the 211 participants with each participant's three observations kept together. Encoder and scaling contrasts are paired differences under the same resampling. A within-participant label permutation null (200 permutations) is computed for the pre-stimulus mean encoder.

On ERP CORE the test statistic is the one pre-registered for this mechanism in the public dissertation repository \cite{repo_disso} before any ERP CORE result was computed: the 28-channel pattern of each 15.6~ms bin is decoded separately, and accuracy is averaged over the bins centered in $[-200,0)$~ms. In the \emph{across-participant} regime trials are scaled, then averaged per participant and condition, giving 80 observations per paradigm, and each bin is decoded with participant-grouped five-fold cross-validation repeated five times, standardization, and an L2 logistic readout. Significance comes from 200 permutations that swap each participant's two labels with probability one half. Averaging first and scaling the average, the order of the SHAPE pipeline, is reported as a secondary analysis. In the \emph{within-participant} regime single trials are decoded per bin with shrinkage linear discriminant analysis under stratified five-fold cross-validation repeated twice, and the participant means of the statistic are tested against 0.5 with a one-sample $t$-test. All participants are retained.

\subsection{Averaging-Law Experiment}
To test \eqref{eq:pred}, trials of each ERP CORE participant are first split into five stratified folds. Within each fold, 40 pseudo-trials per class are formed separately from the training and test pools by averaging $k\in\{1,2,4,8,16\}$ distinct trials, so no trial contributes to both sides. Pseudo-trials are scaled and decoded with the same per-bin statistic and shrinkage LDA. The prediction for $k>1$ uses only the observed $k=1$ accuracy. The predictions for the ERP CORE, averaging-law, and EEGNet experiments, including \eqref{eq:pred}, were committed to the public code repository \cite{repo_sequel} before those experiments were run.
