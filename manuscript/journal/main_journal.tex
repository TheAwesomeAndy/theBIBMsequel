\begin{abstract}
How representations of affective event-related potentials (ERPs) degrade under perturbation depends on what an evaluation lets stand for nothing: the number replacing a missing electrode, the origin of each feature coordinate, and what the pre-stimulus baseline holds after normalization. We test each on a fixed leaky integrate-and-fire spiking reservoir, band-power, ERP-window features, and EEGNet, with subject-grouped validation on SHAPE ($211$ subjects, reanalyzed) and a public IAPS cohort ($228$ subjects). Moving the fill of dropped channels along the standardized axis leaves clean predictions unchanged up to solver tolerance yet costs SHAPE ERP-window features up to $\V{sj-o30-k0minuskm30-erp-abs}$ BA at $30\%$ dropout, and the zero-versus-mean choice rewrites labels in the order of feature non-centeredness ($\V{sj-flip-band}$, $\V{sj-flip-erp}$, and $\V{sj-flip-res}\%$). Per-epoch z-scoring writes a scaled post-stimulus mean into the baseline. On SHAPE the $200$~ms before onset classify affect at $\V{sj-pre-erp-preonly-Z}$ BA (permutation $p=\V{sj-pnull-Z-p}$, $\V{sj-pre-erp-preonly-G}$ under a fixed scale), and the conference reservoir window, mostly pre-stimulus, reads that offset ($R^2=\V{sz-Z-conference-r2}$) and falls to chance under a fixed scale. Placed after onset, the reservoir reaches $\V{sp-clean-res}$, not distinguishable from ERP-window features, and its six bins beat a single count by $\V{sp-bsc6minus1-clean}$. Across ten reservoir draws the measured edge of stability lies near $\rho^\ast=\V{se-rhostar-mean}$ on both cohorts, below $\rho=1$, and the firing threshold moves it from $\V{se-rhostar-theta0.25}$ to beyond $\V{se-rhostar-theta1.0}$. Clean accuracy is broad around it, but with an onset window silent electrodes cost $\V{seo-loss30-high-min}$ to $\V{seo-loss30-high-max}$ BA past it, against at most $\V{seo-loss30-low-max}$ below. We recommend reporting fill sweeps, pre-stimulus decoding, and operating points across draws.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Electroencephalographic event-related potentials (ERPs) measure evoked neural dynamics at millisecond resolution, but the recorded waveform is a noisy, low-dimensional, and subject-dependent projection of an unobserved neural process~\cite{makeig2002dynamic,blankertz2011singletrial}. A representation of affective ERPs can separate conditions under one protocol and keep little usable structure after amplitude noise, temporal misalignment, or electrode loss, and endpoint accuracy alone does not say which. Spiking reservoirs make the gap sharper: fixed recurrent networks give nonlinear temporal expansions~\cite{maass2002real,lukosevicius2009reservoir}, yet EEG studies usually score them as classifiers~\cite{gong2023sglnet,craik2019deep} rather than as measurement operators whose invariances can be tested.

Every such test has to decide what \emph{nothing} means. An absent electrode must be represented by some number. A feature coordinate has an origin, and a zero-filled block sits at that origin whether or not it means anything there. The interval before stimulus onset contains no stimulus-locked signal, yet it is part of every epoch the encoder reads. A score has to be compared with the floor set by no information at all. And a reservoir's recurrence can be switched off ($\rho=0$) to ask what the dynamics add. These choices are rarely stated, and it has not been tested how far each one moves a measured result or whether its effect transfers between cohorts.

Our conference study~\cite{lane2026edge} measured two of them on one cohort. On SHAPE ($211$ subjects), raw retention rewarded the encoder closest to chance, and zero-filling a dropped channel before standardization, which is not invariant to re-centering the features (Proposition~1), halved the measured lead of ERP-window features over a near-centered spiking-reservoir code at $30\%$ dropout ($\V{s-fc30-zero-erpres}$ versus $\V{s-fc30-mean-erpres}$ to $\V{s-fc30-spatial-erpres}$ BA), and on DEAP the fill rule alone moved band-power BA by up to $\V{d-maxgap}$. The reservoir, a fixed leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) network read out through six spike-count bins (\BSC{}), sat just past its measured edge of stability ($\rho^\ast\approx0.82$, below the echo-state heuristic $\rho=1$), and removing its recurrence cost $\V{s-rho0-minus-09-abs}$ BA. That study left five questions open. It used one cohort, one reservoir draw, and one EEGNet recipe. It argued Proposition~1 algebraically but never moved the origin to watch the score follow. And its own camera-ready code check found that its reservoir window, $t\in[10,70)$ samples, spans about $-160$ to $+73$~ms around onset, so most of what the reservoir encoded came before the stimulus.

This paper answers those questions on SHAPE itself, rerun from its data with new experiments, and on a second, public cohort. The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A rerun of SHAPE that reproduces the conference's fixed-encoder results exactly and adds analysis windows measured from true onset, and an external, paradigm-matched test on an independent IAPS cohort ($228$ subjects, different laboratory and montage) at the conference-matched epoch and at a long epoch pre-specified from the dataset's published effect latencies (Secs.~\ref{sec:data}, \ref{sec:res-clean}).
    \item A direct experimental test of Proposition~1 on both cohorts: shifting the coordinate origin leaves clean predictions unchanged up to solver tolerance, while the dropout score falls when the fill moves far from the training mean (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-origin}).
    \item Proposition~2, which shows that per-epoch z-scoring writes a function of the post-stimulus signal into the pre-stimulus interval, and the test it implies: on SHAPE the conference reservoir window read that offset rather than evoked dynamics, and the same reservoir placed after onset measures the evoked signal (Secs.~\ref{sec:prop2}, \ref{sec:res-prestim}).
    \item The operating point across ten reservoir draws on SHAPE and on the external drives (five draws for external accuracy), and across thresholds and leaks: where the measured edge of stability lies and whether accuracy follows it, judged by a decision rule fixed before the SHAPE sweep ran (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-edge}).
    \item Two physically grounded perturbations, spherical-spline repair of removed electrodes and re-averaging from fewer recorded trials, together with EEGNet with and without augmentation and across recipes on the second cohort (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-signal}).
\end{enumerate}
The affective categories define the task. The object of study is what each representation does with nothing: with a missing electrode, a shifted origin, an empty baseline, and a chance floor.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\emph{ERP representations of affect.} Emotional pictures enlarge the late positive potential relative to neutral ones from roughly $400$~ms after onset~\cite{cuthbert2000brain,schupp2006emotion,hajcak2010erp}, and a recent cohort of $224$ participants adds an ordered valence effect between $1500$ and $2500$~ms~\cite{revers2025dissociating}. ERP/EEG decoders commonly use window amplitudes, Hjorth~\cite{hjorth1970eeg} and spectral descriptors~\cite{subasi2007eeg}, and linear models~\cite{blankertz2011singletrial,grootswagers2017decoding}, which are interpretable through these known components but can miss nonlinear temporal structure. Deep networks such as EEGNet~\cite{lawhern2018eegnet} are now common~\cite{craik2019deep,roy2019deep}, and much of this literature reports endpoint accuracy over preprocessing and acquisition choices, which can change measured performance materially~\cite{delpup2025preprocessing,chiang2023depression,guo2025mma}.

\emph{Reservoirs and the edge of stability.} Reservoir computing maps an input through a fixed high-dimensional recurrent system to a state trajectory read out by a simple classifier~\cite{maass2002real,jaeger2001echo,jaeger2004harnessing,lukosevicius2009reservoir}, and spiking liquid-state machines add event timing and count structure~\cite{diehl2015unsupervised,roy2019towards}. Computation is often reported to peak near the transition between ordered and irregular dynamics, located by how far a small state perturbation spreads~\cite{bertschinger2004realtime,legenstein2007edge,boedecker2012information}. The spectral radius $\rho=1$ is a heuristic for rate reservoirs, and it is neither necessary nor sufficient for the echo-state property of a driven system~\cite{yildiz2012revisiting}, so for a thresholded spiking reservoir the transition need not lie at $\rho=1$. Neuromorphic reservoir features for affective EEG were introduced in earlier ARSPI-Net work~\cite{lane2023arspi,lane2024arspi}, and this paper extends our conference study~\cite{lane2026edge}.

\emph{Missing electrodes.} Dynamic spatial filtering handles missing channels with a learned attention front end~\cite{banville2022dsf}, and channel-dropout or noise augmentation hardens trained models~\cite{rommel2022augment}. A recent EEG foundation-model benchmark finds that apparent channel-dropout fragility depends on whether dropped channels are removed or zero-padded~\cite{sirca2026beyond}, and an evaluation of four EEG decoding models finds channel permutation far more damaging than channel removal~\cite{wang2026evaluation}. Both observe that the representation of an absent or misplaced channel moves the measured result. Proposition~1 below gives the mechanism for standardized features and the condition under which a fill is harmless. Fills range from constants and spherical-spline interpolation~\cite{perrin1989spherical}, through learned imputation~\cite{banville2022dsf}, to generative synthesis of whole missing modalities~\cite{bi2024graymatters,hu2025gmldm}. The non-learned fills studied here sit at the simple end of that spectrum.

\emph{Evaluation choices that create or remove effects.} Filtering can move late ERP effects to earlier latencies and create artifactual early effects~\cite{acunzo2012systematic,tanner2015inappropriate,vanrullen2011four}, and leakage between preprocessing and evaluation inflates reported accuracy across machine-learning science~\cite{kriegeskorte2009circular,kapoor2023leakage}. Decoding accuracy near a theoretical chance level needs empirical null distributions~\cite{combrisson2015exceeding}, and cross-validated accuracy on a few hundred observations carries wide error bars~\cite{varoquaux2018cross}. Per-epoch normalization, the fill convention for an absent electrode, and the origin of a feature coordinate belong to the same family of choices. We give each a proposition or a direct test.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{ERP Observation Model}
Let $X_s^{(c)}\in\R^{C\times T}$ denote the trial-averaged ERP observation of subject $s$ in condition $c$. We treat it as a noisy readout of an unobserved neural state trajectory,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obs}
    \xi_{t+1}=f_\theta(\xi_t,u_t)+\eta_t,\qquad
    x_t=H\xi_t+\epsilon_t,
\end{equation}
where $H$ is the scalp measurement operator and $\epsilon_t$ collects measurement noise, residual single-trial variability, and preprocessing. Every perturbation below acts on a term of this model: electrode removal zeroes rows of $H$, additive noise and re-averaging from fewer trials enlarge $\epsilon_t$, and alignment jitter shifts the sampling index of $x_t$. Normalization acts on $x_t$ before any encoder sees it, which is why it is treated as part of the measurement (Sec.~\ref{sec:prop2}).

\subsection{Spiking Reservoir Encoding}
Each electrode trace drives a fixed LIF reservoir of $256$ units with input weights $W_{\mathrm{in}}$, recurrent weights $W$, leak $\beta=0.05$, and threshold $\vartheta=0.5$:
\begin{align}
    v_{t} &= \big[(1-\beta)\,(1-z_{t-1})\odot v_{t-1} + W z_{t-1} + W_{\mathrm{in}}x_t\big]_+, \\
    z_{t} &= \Ind[v_{t}\geq \vartheta],
\end{align}
where $v_t$ is the membrane potential, $z_t$ the binary spike vector, and $[\cdot]_+$ rectification at zero. The factor $(1-z_{t-1})$ resets every unit that spiked at the previous step. $W_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $W$ are drawn once from a Xavier-uniform law, and $W$ is rescaled to spectral radius $\rho$. Unless stated otherwise the draw is seed $42$ and $\rho=0.9$, the conference operating point, and only the readout is trained. Figure~\ref{fig:resdyn} shows the reservoir's activity and the conference operating-point results on SHAPE.

Activity is summarized by six equal temporal bins over an analysis window. For unit $j$ and bin $k$, the binned spike-count code is $b_{j,k}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}_k} z_j(t)$. Each electrode's $1536$-dimensional spike-bin vector is projected onto a single $64$-component PCA basis shared across electrodes and fitted on the training subjects of each fold, and the projections are concatenated, giving $E\in\R^{64C}$ ($2176$ for SHAPE, $2048$ for the external cohort):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operator}
E=\phi_{\mathrm{PCA}}\!\big([b_1,\dots,b_6]\big)=\Phi(X).
\end{equation}
A companion study compares this data-fitted projection with a fixed random projection and examines how subject covariance shapes affective EEG embeddings~\cite{lane2026entanglement}. Here we keep the conference's train-only PCA so that every result is comparable with the conference version.
The conference window is $t\in[10,70)$ in samples. With onset at sample $51$ in both cohorts it spans about $-160$ to $+73$~ms around onset, so most of it precedes the stimulus. Section~\ref{sec:prop2} examines what that window can read, and Sec.~\ref{sec:res-prestim} adds windows wholly before and wholly after onset. We also rerun the conference analyses with windows measured from true onset, both fixed before any of their results was computed: the window that the submitted conference manuscript described before its camera-ready correction, $+39$ to $+273$~ms ($t\in[61,121)$, the \emph{onset} window), and the whole post-onset interval, $0$ to $+797$~ms ($t\in[51,255)$, six bins of $34$ samples, the \emph{post} window). In both reruns the ERP-window encoder uses its three windows measured from onset ($80$--$200$, $250$--$450$, and $450$--$800$~ms), as the submitted manuscript labeled them.

A silent electrode is a fixed point of the reservoir: with $x_t=0$ from a zero initial state, $v_t$ stays at zero and no unit spikes, so the electrode's spike-bin block is exactly zero. After the PCA step that block maps to $-P\bar b$, the negative of the projected training mean, not to the origin of the embedding.

\subsection{Operating Point and Order Parameter}
To test whether the recurrent dynamics matter, the same draw is rescaled to $\rho\in\{0,0.3,0.6,0.9,1.2,1.5\}$ with every other parameter fixed ($\rho=0$ leaves a feedforward LIF layer). A label-free order parameter locates the dynamical transition by damage spreading~\cite{bertschinger2004realtime}: two copies of the reservoir receive the same electrode drive, one unit's spike is flipped at $t=10$, and the normalized Hamming distance between the copies' spike vectors is averaged over steps $21$ to $69$ and over $400$ sampled drives and flipped units. The transition $\rho^\ast$ is the $\rho$ at which this damage, linearly interpolated on the grid $\rho=0,0.1,\dots,2$, reaches half its maximum. The conference measured $\rho^\ast$ and accuracy for one draw on SHAPE. Here both are repeated for ten independent draws (seeds $42$--$51$) on SHAPE with the conference definition, accuracy clean and with $30\%$ of the electrodes silent, and the accuracy sweep is repeated with the onset and post windows. On the external cohort the order parameter uses ten draws on the drives of the conference-matched epoch, and the accuracy sweep five draws (seeds $42$--$46$) on the long epoch (Sec.~\ref{sec:data}), where the reservoir is above chance. For draw $42$ the transition is also measured at thresholds $\vartheta\in\{0.25,0.5,1.0\}$ and leaks $\beta\in\{0.02,0.05,0.1,0.2\}$ on both cohorts' drives. Before the SHAPE sweep ran we fixed the decision rule that the conference review proposed: the recurrence effect stands if the deficit at $\rho=0$ relative to $\rho=0.9$ keeps its sign in at least nine of ten draws and the interval of the draw-mean difference, computed on the same subject resamples for every draw, excludes zero. Otherwise only a broad optimum is claimed.

\subsection{Baselines}
Band-power uses Welch power in five bands ($1$--$4$, $4$--$8$, $8$--$13$, $13$--$30$, $30$--$45$~Hz) per channel ($5C$ dimensions). ERP-window uses mean amplitude in three windows, about $-120$--$0$~ms, $50$--$250$~ms, and $250$--$600$~ms relative to onset, per channel ($3C$ dimensions). All three fixed encoders feed a balanced L2-regularized logistic readout. As a trained baseline, EEGNet-8,2~\cite{lawhern2018eegnet} is trained per fold on the same per-epoch z-scored $C\times T$ signal that the fixed encoders read (Adam, learning rate $10^{-3}$, $80$ epochs, batch $64$, dropout $0.25$) with train-only per-channel standardization, with and without augmentation by random channel dropout and additive noise. On the external cohort the recipe is also varied ($40$ or $160$ epochs, dropout $0.5$, conference-matched epoch) to probe the effect of a single recipe. For cost, the recipe variants and EEGNet on the long epoch use one partition (seed $42$, five folds) instead of five, with intervals from the same subject bootstrap.

\subsection{Perturbations}
\label{sec:perturb-methods}
The feature-coordinate experiment suppresses a dropped channel's feature block after extraction and refills it by one of four rules: zero, train mean, train-fitted $k$-nearest-neighbor imputation ($k{=}10$), and the mean of the retained channels (spatial). It isolates the fill convention and applies to the fixed encoders.

The signal-level experiment corrupts the test subjects' signal before any feature extraction, identically for every encoder including EEGNet, with the same draws in each fold, while training uses the clean signal: removal of $10$, $30$, or $50\%$ of the electrodes (a removed electrode is a zero trace), additive white noise at $5$~dB SNR per channel, and a uniform alignment shift of up to $\pm50$~ms. Two signal-level conditions are new. \emph{Spline repair} replaces each removed electrode by spherical-spline interpolation from the retained electrodes~\cite{perrin1989spherical} (order $4$, seven Legendre terms) on the baseline-corrected microvolt signal before normalization, a physically grounded alternative to the zero trace. \emph{Recorded observation noise} re-averages every test observation from $1$, $4$, or $16$ of its own single trials instead of all of them, so the added variance is the recorded trial-to-trial variability rather than synthetic noise. It needs single trials and is run on the external cohort only.

\subsection{Retention and the Coordinate Origin}
\label{sec:confound}
\emph{Raw retention}, the ratio of perturbed to clean BA, overstates the stability of representations whose clean BA is close to chance ($c=1/3$), so we also report \emph{above-chance retention}, $(\mathrm{BA}_{\mathrm{pert}}-c)/(\mathrm{BA}_{\mathrm{clean}}-c)$, and treat absolute BA and paired subject-level differences as primary.

Write the standardized readout input as $z=D_\sigma^{-1}(x-\mu)$ with $D_\sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma)$, and let $S$ index the dropped coordinates. Zero-fill sets $x_S=0$, so the dropped block enters as $z_S=-D_{\sigma,S}^{-1}\mu_S$, and train-mean fill sets $x_S=\mu_S$, so $z_S=0$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noninv}
z_S^{\mathrm{zero}}-z_S^{\mathrm{mean}}=-D_{\sigma,S}^{-1}\mu_S .
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1 (origin dependence of zero-fill).} \emph{Let the clean readout depend on $x$ only through $z$, so it is invariant to a constant offset $x\mapsto x-b$ (train-only standardization guarantees this). Then the clean prediction is unchanged by the re-centering, while the zero-fill dropout input shifts by $\Delta z_S=D_{\sigma,S}^{-1}b_S$. Hence the measured zero-fill dropout accuracy depends on the coordinate origin, and the zero- and mean-fill inputs coincide if and only if $\mu_S=0$.}

\noindent\emph{Proof.} Standardization subtracts the training mean, so $b$ cancels from the clean input. A zero-filled coordinate maps to $z_i=-(\mu_i-b_i)/\sigma_i$ instead of $-\mu_i/\sigma_i$, a shift of $b_i/\sigma_i\neq0$ for any $b_i\neq0$.\hfill$\square$

For a linear readout with class weights $W$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:noninv} moves the class logits by $\Delta\ell=-W_S D_{\sigma,S}^{-1}\mu_S$ between the two fills, so the fills can disagree only as far as the dropped features are non-centered. Proposition~1 has a direct experimental test, which the conference study did not run: fill the dropped block with the raw value $x_i=\mu_i+\kappa\sigma_i$, which places it at standardized position $z_i=\kappa$ for every encoder, and sweep $\kappa$. Clean predictions must not change with any offset $b$, while dropout accuracy can depend on $\kappa$, with train-mean fill at $\kappa=0$. An encoder's own zero places each dropped feature at its own $z_i=-\mu_i/\sigma_i$, at a mean absolute standardized distance $\overline{|\mu|/\sigma}$ from the training mean, so it is not a single point on the $\kappa$ axis. We refit the readout on features shifted by $b=\mu+2\sigma$ to check the first prediction and sweep $\kappa\in[-3,3]$ to test the second.

\subsection{What the Pre-Stimulus Interval Can Contain}
\label{sec:prop2}
Both cohorts are baseline-corrected over the $200$~ms before onset, so the pre-stimulus mean of every trial-averaged trace is zero at the recording rate and close to zero after decimation, and both are then z-scored per channel over the whole epoch, as in the conference pipeline. Let $P$ be the pre-stimulus samples and $Q$ the rest, with $|P|+|Q|=T$, and let $m_Q$ be the post-stimulus mean and $s$ the epoch standard deviation of one channel.

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 2 (baseline transfer under per-epoch normalization).} \emph{For a trace with zero pre-stimulus mean normalized by its own epoch mean and standard deviation, the mean of the normalized pre-stimulus segment is $-\tfrac{|Q|}{T}\,m_Q/s$. The pre-stimulus segment is therefore a deterministic function of the post-stimulus signal, and any encoder that reads only $P$ has access to $m_Q/s$.}

\noindent\emph{Proof.} The epoch mean is $(|P|\cdot 0+|Q|\,m_Q)/T$. Subtracting it and dividing by $s$ maps the zero pre-stimulus mean to $-|Q|m_Q/(Ts)$.\hfill$\square$

With onset at sample $51$ of $256$, $|Q|/T=0.80$. An affective modulation of the late positive potential changes $m_Q$, so under per-epoch z-scoring a classifier can separate conditions from an interval in which no stimulus has yet been shown. A fixed per-channel scale (one standard deviation per channel, pooled over all observations without labels and applied identically to every epoch) leaves the pre-stimulus mean where baseline correction put it and removes this route. Because the scale is shared by all epochs, it cannot carry information from one part of an epoch into another, although it is transductive with respect to subject inclusion. We compare the two normalizations for windows wholly before onset, the conference window, and windows wholly after onset (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-prestim}). Proposition~2 also says what a reservoir window before onset can encode. Its drive there is nearly constant at the transferred offset, and a fixed reservoir driven by a nearly constant input encodes that constant in its spike counts. We test this directly. For every trace we predict the post-stimulus mean $m_Q/s$ from the spike-bin code of a window wholly before onset ($t\in[3,51)$) and of the conference window, with a ridge regression (penalty $1$) fitted on four-fifths of the subjects and scored by out-of-fold $R^2$, under per-epoch z-scoring and under the fixed scale. We also report the per-channel correlation between the total spike count in the conference window and $m_Q/s$, and with $|m_Q/s|$, because the rectified membrane and input weights of both signs let an offset of either sign raise the count.

\section{Data and Protocol}
\label{sec:data}
\subsection{SHAPE Cohort}
The primary cohort is the trial-averaged affective ERP data of the Stress, Health, and the Psychophysiology of Emotion (SHAPE) project~\cite{shape}, collected by the Laboratory for Clinical Affective Neuroscience at Stony Brook University: $211$ subjects after exclusion of one anomalous record, each contributing negative, neutral, and pleasant observations ($633$ in total). Each epoch ($34$ scalp channels at $1024$~Hz, $1.2$~s, baseline-corrected over the $200$~ms pre-stimulus interval) is analyzed as every fourth of its first $1024$ samples: $256$ samples at $256$~Hz spanning $-200$ to $+800$~ms, onset at sample $51$, z-scored per channel within each epoch (Fig.~\ref{fig:erp}). For this version every new experiment that subject averages and an undocumented montage allow was also run on SHAPE, except EEGNet. We checked that the conference input equals the z-scored, every-fourth-sample microvolt epoch to the last bit for all $633$ observations, and the journal code reproduces exactly the conference values it repeats: clean BA and intervals of the three fixed encoders, all four fills at $30\%$ dropout, and the draw-$42$ accuracy sweep and order parameter ($\V{sj-repro-n}$ values, largest difference $\V{sj-repro-maxdiff}$, reservoir BA $\V{sj-clean-res}$, CI $\V{sj-clean-res-ci}$). The pooled-basis sensitivity check differs from the conference value ($0.485$) by one observation of $633$ ($\V{sj-pooledpca}$), because the conference used an embedding regenerated by an earlier script with a different numerical path. The microvolt epochs give the fixed-scale normalization of Sec.~\ref{sec:prop2}. The channel order of the SHAPE export is not documented, so no montage-dependent analysis (spline repair) runs on SHAPE, and only subject averages exist, so recorded trial-count noise is measured on the external cohort alone. SHAPE data are held in a restricted environment, and only aggregate results leave it.

\subsection{External Cohort}
The external cohort is a public IAPS passive-viewing dataset~\cite{revers2025dissociating,tcrzem2025data} from a different laboratory and montage: $32$ scalp electrodes (BioSemi layout) re-referenced to the mastoids and exported at $512$~Hz after the dataset authors' topographic interpolation of bad channels, filtering, ICA ocular correction, and artifact rejection. Of $239$ participants, the $228$ with a complete negative, neutral, and pleasant panel in the preprocessed release are used ($684$ observations, median $44$ trials per observation, range $20$ to $54$, with high- and low-arousal pictures of one valence pooled). Each observation is built to the SHAPE layout: trial average, baseline correction over $-200$ to $0$~ms, every second sample from $-200$~ms, giving $256$ samples at $256$~Hz with onset at sample $51$, and z-scoring per channel within the epoch. This conference-matched epoch is the primary external analysis. The grand averages reproduce the published effects (Fig.~\ref{fig:tcrzem-erp}): emotional pictures exceed neutral ones by $2.12$~$\mu$V at PO3 between $500$ and $1300$~ms, and negative pictures exceed pleasant ones by $1.32$~$\mu$V between $1500$ and $2500$~ms. Most of both effects lies after $+800$~ms, so we pre-specified, before computing any of its results, a secondary analysis on a long epoch from $-200$ to $+2496$~ms ($691$ samples, same sampling, baseline, and onset). In it the reservoir window covers $0$ to $+2484$~ms after onset (six bins of $106$ samples), the ERP-window encoder adds the two published windows ($500$--$1300$ and $1500$--$2500$~ms), and band-power and EEGNet see the whole epoch.

\subsection{DEAP Check}
On DEAP~\cite{koelstra2012deap} ($32$ subjects, band-power features, binary valence, within-subject $5$-fold) the conference measured how much the fill rule alone moves channel-dropout accuracy. That check is reported unchanged (Fig.~\ref{fig:deapaug}a).

\subsection{Validation}
All analyses share one fold structure: StratifiedGroupKFold ($5$ folds, seeds $42$--$46$) with subjects as groups, so a subject's observations never span training and test. Standardization and the reservoir's PCA basis are fitted on the training subjects of each fold, the fixed encoders use a balanced L2 logistic readout, and EEGNet is trained on the same folds. The out-of-fold class probabilities of each observation are averaged over the five partitions, and $95\%$ confidence intervals (CIs) come from $4000$ resamples of the subjects with replacement, each keeping its three observations. Contrasts between encoders, fills, conditions, or operating points are paired differences under the same resampling, so no test treats the $25$ overlapping folds as independent. Chance is $1/3$, and a within-subject label-permutation null ($20$ permutations, in which each subject keeps one observation per class) gives the empirical floor for every fixed encoder~\cite{combrisson2015exceeding}. The pre-stimulus test of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-prestim} uses $\V{xl-pnull-n}$ such permutations and reports $p=(1+\#\{\text{null}\geq\text{observed}\})/(1+\V{xl-pnull-n})$. The journal code reproduces the conference implementation bit for bit, on synthetic data (reservoir codes, damage, and bootstrap intervals) and on SHAPE.

\subsection{Feature-Coordinate Fills: What Replicates}
\label{sec:res-fills}
Proposition~1 names the input-level cause of the conference result: the zero-filled block sits at $-\mu_S/\sigma_S$ in standardized coordinates. That cause replicates almost exactly. The mean $|\mu|/\sigma$ of the features is $\V{s-cent-band}$, $\V{s-cent-erp}$, and $\V{s-cent-res}$ for band-power, ERP-window, and the reservoir on SHAPE, and $\V{xl-cent-band}$, $\V{xl-cent-erp}$, and $\V{xl-cent-res}$ on the external long epoch ($\V{x-cent-band}$, $\V{x-cent-erp}$, $\V{x-cent-res}$ at the matched epoch). The near-centeredness of the reservoir belongs to its PCA step, not to its spikes: the raw spike-count code is about as far from centered as band-power at the matched epoch ($\V{x-cent-rawcounts}$ versus $\V{x-cent-band}$) and farther on the long window ($\V{xl-cent-rawcounts}$), and the shared basis subtracts the pooled mean.

The accuracy consequence depends on the window and the cohort (Table~\ref{tab:fills}, Fig.~\ref{fig:fills}). On SHAPE with the conference windows, train-mean fill exceeded zero-fill at $30\%$ dropout by $\V{s-meanzero30-band}$ BA for band-power and $\V{s-meanzero30-erp}$ for ERP-window, and zero-fill halved the ERP-window lead over the reservoir (the rerun reproduces these values exactly). With the ERP windows measured from true onset, the band-power contrast is unchanged, since band-power reads the whole epoch, but the ERP-window contrast vanishes ($\V{so-fmz-mean30-erp}$, CI $\V{so-fmz-mean30-erp-ci}$), and the ERP-window lead over the reservoir at $30\%$ dropout is $\V{so-fc30-zero-erpres}$ under zero-fill and $\V{so-fc30-mean-erpres}$ under mean fill (Table~\ref{tab:windows}). The conference halving therefore depended on the conference ERP windows, the first of which lies before onset. On the external long epoch the same contrasts are $\V{xl-fmz-mean30-band}$ ($\V{xl-fmz-mean30-band-ci}$) and $\V{xl-fmz-mean30-erp}$ ($\V{xl-fmz-mean30-erp-ci}$), and no fill moves the ERP-window lead outside noise. What does replicate is the near-centered code's stability under the fill choice: the reservoir's two constant fills differ by at most $\V{x-resfill-maxabs-both}$ BA at every rate from $10$ to $50\%$ on both external epochs, with paired intervals $\V{xl-resfill-wmin}$ to $\V{xl-resfill-wmax}$ BA wide on the long epoch against $\V{xl-nonfill-wmin}$ to $\V{xl-nonfill-wmax}$ for the non-centered encoders. Section~\ref{sec:res-origin} shows why accuracy moved on one cohort and not on the other.

\subsection{Moving the Origin: A Direct Test of Proposition~1}
\label{sec:res-origin}
Proposition~1 makes two predictions that can be tested by moving the coordinate origin. Both hold on SHAPE, where every encoder has accuracy to lose. On the external cohort the first holds on both epochs and the second on the long epoch (Fig.~\ref{fig:origin}, Table~\ref{tab:origin}).

First, clean predictions do not depend on the origin. Refitting every readout on features shifted by $b=\mu+2\sigma$ changes no predicted label for band-power or the reservoir on either cohort. For ERP-window it changes at most $\V{sj-invlabels-erp}\%$ of the labels in any fold on SHAPE and $\V{xl-invlabels-erp}\%$ on the external long epoch, near-ties that the stopping tolerance of the L-BFGS solver resolves differently, and class probabilities differ by at most $\V{sj-inv-erp}$ and $\V{xl-inv-erp}$ (the reservoir's agree to $\V{sj-inv-res}$).

Second, the dropout score depends on where the fill sits. On SHAPE, with $30\%$ of the electrodes dropped, ERP-window BA is $\V{sj-o30-kp00-erp}$ with the fill at the training mean ($\kappa=0$) but $\V{sj-o30-kp30-erp}$ and $\V{sj-o30-km30-erp}$ with the same block three standard deviations above or below it, paired losses of $\V{sj-o30-k0minuskp30-erp-abs}$ (CI $\V{sj-o30-k0minuskp30-erp-ci}$) and $\V{sj-o30-k0minuskm30-erp-abs}$ (CI $\V{sj-o30-k0minuskm30-erp-ci}$), and its loss already excludes zero at $|\kappa|=1$. Band-power falls from $\V{sj-o30-kp00-band}$ to $\V{sj-o30-kp30-band}$ and $\V{sj-o30-km30-band}$, again with losses at every $|\kappa|\geq1$. The reservoir moves least. It loses $\V{sj-o30-k0minuskp20-res-abs}$ at $\kappa=+2$ (CI $\V{sj-o30-k0minuskp20-res-ci}$) and $\V{sj-o30-k0minuskp30-res-abs}$ at $\kappa=+3$ (CI $\V{sj-o30-k0minuskp30-res-ci}$), and no contrast at negative $\kappa$ excludes zero. The conference's zero-versus-mean comparison is one step along this axis. Each encoder's native zero places its dropped features at a mean absolute standardized distance of $\V{sj-zpos-band}$ (band-power), $\V{sj-zpos-erp}$ (ERP-window), and $\V{sj-zpos-res}$ (reservoir) from the training mean, and costs $\V{sj-o30-k0minusnative_zero-band}$ (CI $\V{sj-o30-k0minusnative_zero-band-ci}$), $\V{sj-o30-k0minusnative_zero-erp}$ (CI $\V{sj-o30-k0minusnative_zero-erp-ci}$), and $\V{sj-o30-k0minusnative_zero-res}$ (CI $\V{sj-o30-k0minusnative_zero-res-ci}$) BA against train-mean fill. The reservoir's feature-coordinate zero (its PCA origin) lies on average $\V{sj-zpos-res}$ standard deviations from its training mean, which is why the fill choice was immaterial for it.

With the windows measured from onset the ERP-window sweep keeps its form ($\V{so-o30-kp00-erp}$ at $\kappa=0$, $\V{so-o30-kp30-erp}$ and $\V{so-o30-km30-erp}$ at $\kappa=\pm3$), and the reservoir now loses in both directions ($\V{so-o30-k0minuskm30-res-abs}$ at $\kappa=-3$, CI $\V{so-o30-k0minuskm30-res-ci}$, and $\V{so-o30-k0minuskp30-res-abs}$ at $\kappa=+3$, CI $\V{so-o30-k0minuskp30-res-ci}$). With the post window the reservoir loses about as much as ERP-window at $\kappa=\pm3$ ($\V{sp-o30-k0minuskp30-res-abs}$ and $\V{sp-o30-k0minuskm30-res-abs}$, against $\V{sp-o30-k0minuskp30-erp-abs}$ and $\V{sp-o30-k0minuskm30-erp-abs}$), so its small sensitivity with the conference window is not a fixed property of the reservoir code. ERP-window's native zero, however, now costs nothing against mean fill ($\V{so-o30-k0minusnative_zero-erp}$, CI $\V{so-o30-k0minusnative_zero-erp-ci}$) although it changes $\V{so-flip-erp}\%$ of the labels. Proposition~1 predicts that a fill moves the input and therefore the labels. Whether the moved labels are more often wrong depends on where the shift sends observations relative to the decision boundaries, which a sweep over $\kappa$ shows and a single zero-versus-mean comparison does not.

The external long epoch shows the same form at smaller size. ERP-window BA is $\V{xl-o30-kp00-erp}$ at $\kappa=0$ and $\V{xl-o30-kp30-erp}$ at $\kappa=+3$, a loss of $\V{xl-o30-k0minuskp30-erp-abs}$ (CI $\V{xl-o30-k0minuskp30-erp-ci}$), and ERP-window also loses at $\kappa=\pm2$. Band-power and the reservoir lose only at $\kappa=+3$ ($\V{xl-o30-k0minuskp30-band-abs}$, CI $\V{xl-o30-k0minuskp30-band-ci}$, and $\V{xl-o30-k0minuskp30-res-abs}$, CI $\V{xl-o30-k0minuskp30-res-ci}$). At the conference-matched epoch, where the encoders are near chance, no $\kappa$ contrast at $30\%$ dropout excludes zero.

The mechanism is the same on both cohorts even where accuracy cannot move. Between zero-fill and mean-fill, the class logits of a test observation move by $\lVert\Delta\ell\rVert=\V{sj-dlogit-band}$ for band-power, $\V{sj-dlogit-erp}$ for ERP-window, and $\V{sj-dlogit-res}$ for the reservoir on SHAPE (class-centered norm, mean over folds), and the fill choice changes the predicted label of $\V{sj-flip-band}\%$, $\V{sj-flip-erp}\%$, and $\V{sj-flip-res}\%$ of the test observations, in the order of the encoders' non-centeredness. The external cohort gives the same order on the long epoch ($\V{xl-flip-band}\%$, $\V{xl-flip-erp}\%$, $\V{xl-flip-res}\%$) and at the matched epoch ($\V{x-flip-band}\%$, $\V{x-flip-erp}\%$, $\V{x-flip-res}\%$). On SHAPE with the conference windows those label changes cost accuracy. On the external cohort they rewrite about a third of the non-centered encoders' labels yet cost little, consistent with the encoders having about a third of SHAPE's above-chance margin to lose ($\V{xl-marginratio-erp}$ for ERP-window, $\V{xl-marginratio-band}$ for band-power). Proposition~1 thus predicts which representations a fill rule perturbs, and the above-chance margin limits how much accuracy that perturbation can remove, although the onset-window ERP encoder shows that a large margin does not guarantee a cost.

\subsection{The Empty Baseline}
\label{sec:res-prestim}
Proposition~2 predicts that under per-epoch z-scoring the pre-stimulus mean of each normalized trace is a scaled negative of its post-stimulus mean. The correlation between the two is $\V{sj-prestim-corr}$ across the $633\times34$ SHAPE traces (post-stimulus mean in microvolts), and $\V{x-prestim-corr}$ and $\V{xl-prestim-corr}$ across the $684\times32$ external traces at the matched and long epochs, so the transfer happens on both cohorts. Whether it carries condition information depends on how much the post-stimulus mean differs between conditions (Fig.~\ref{fig:prestim}).

On SHAPE it carries a great deal. ERP-window features computed only from the $200$~ms before onset reach BA $\V{sj-pre-erp-preonly-Z}$ $\V{sj-pre-erp-preonly-Z-ci}$ under per-epoch z-scoring (permutation $p=\V{sj-pnull-Z-p}$, the smallest value $\V{sj-pnull-n}$ permutations can give, with $95$th null percentile $\V{sj-pnull-Z-p95}$) and $\V{sj-pre-erp-preonly-G}$ $\V{sj-pre-erp-preonly-G-ci}$ under the fixed per-channel scale, a paired difference of $\V{sj-pre-erp-preonly-ZminusG}$ (CI $\V{sj-pre-erp-preonly-ZminusG-ci}$). Samples recorded before the picture appeared thus classify affect far above chance once each epoch is normalized by its own mean and standard deviation. The first window of the conference ERP-window encoder ($-120$ to $0$~ms) lies inside that interval, and with the fixed scale the encoder falls from $\V{sj-pre-erp-conference-Z}$ to $\V{sj-pre-erp-conference-G}$ (paired $\V{sj-pre-erp-conference-ZminusG}$, CI $\V{sj-pre-erp-conference-ZminusG-ci}$).

The conference reservoir read the same offset. With its conference window ($-160$ to $+73$~ms) it scores $\V{sj-pre-res-conference-Z}$ under z-scoring but $\V{sj-pre-res-conference-G}$ $\V{sj-pre-res-conference-G-ci}$ under the fixed scale, which is chance. Its code reads the offset almost perfectly. Under z-scoring, the spike-bin code of a window wholly before onset predicts each trace's post-stimulus mean with out-of-fold $R^2=\V{sz-Z-pre-r2}$ ($\V{sz-Z-conference-r2}$ for the conference window), and under the fixed scale with $R^2=\V{sz-G-pre-r2}$ ($\V{sz-G-conference-r2}$). The pre-specified per-channel correlation between the total spike count and the signed post-stimulus mean is weak (median $\V{sz-Z-conference-r-med}$ over channels, range $\V{sz-Z-conference-r-min}$ to $\V{sz-Z-conference-r-max}$), but the correlation with its magnitude is strong (median $\V{sz-Z-conference-rabs-med}$): offsets of either sign raise the count, and the count alone predicts the signed mean poorly ($R^2=\V{sz-Z-conference-r2count}$ for the conference window), so the sign is carried by which units fire, not by how many. The external cohort gives the same picture ($R^2=\V{xz-Z-pre-r2}$ under z-scoring against $\V{xz-G-pre-r2}$ under the fixed scale for the pre-stimulus window). The conference accuracy of the reservoir on SHAPE therefore measures the normalization offset, a function of the post-stimulus mean, and not evoked dynamics inside its window. This is consistent with the single-bin control BSC$_1$ matching \BSC{} on the conference window ($\V{sj-bsc6minus1-clean}$ BA), since an offset that is constant within the window has no temporal structure to bin. It is not a failure of the reservoir under the fixed scale: with the same scale it reaches $\V{sj-pre-res-post-G}$ over the whole post-onset interval.

Pointed at the evoked signal, the same reservoir measures it. Over the whole post-onset interval ($0$ to $+797$~ms, same reservoir, same six bins) it reaches $\V{sj-pre-res-post-Z}$ $\V{sj-pre-res-post-Z-ci}$ under z-scoring, a gain of $\V{sj-pre-postminusconf-Z}$ BA over the conference window (CI $\V{sj-pre-postminusconf-Z-ci}$), and $\V{sj-pre-res-post-G}$ $\V{sj-pre-res-post-G-ci}$ under the fixed scale, where the difference between the normalizations no longer excludes zero ($\V{sj-pre-res-post-ZminusG}$, CI $\V{sj-pre-res-post-ZminusG-ci}$). Its accuracy is then not distinguishable from that of the ERP-window encoder with windows measured from onset ($\V{so-clean-erp}$, paired lead $\V{sp-clean-erpres}$, CI $\V{sp-clean-erpres-ci}$). The offset does not vanish at onset, however. The early post-onset window ($0$ to $+234$~ms) reaches $\V{sj-pre-res-earlypost-Z}$ under z-scoring but $\V{sj-pre-res-earlypost-G}$ under the fixed scale (paired $\V{sj-pre-res-earlypost-ZminusG}$, CI $\V{sj-pre-res-earlypost-ZminusG-ci}$), so a z-scored window near onset can still read part of it. Per-epoch normalization can also remove information after onset, but on SHAPE this is not resolved: ERP-window features from the conference's two post-onset windows ($50$--$250$ and $250$--$600$~ms) reach $\V{sj-pre-erp-postonly-G}$ under the fixed scale and $\V{sj-pre-erp-postonly-Z}$ under z-scoring (paired $\V{sj-pre-erp-postonly-ZminusG}$, CI $\V{sj-pre-erp-postonly-ZminusG-ci}$).

The external cohort replicates the direction at smaller size. Pre-stimulus ERP-window features reach $\V{x-pre-erp-preonly-Z}$ $\V{x-pre-erp-preonly-Z-ci}$ under z-scoring at the matched epoch (permutation $p=\V{x-pnull-Z-p}$) and $\V{xl-pre-erp-preonly-Z}$ $\V{xl-pre-erp-preonly-Z-ci}$ on the long epoch ($p=\V{xl-pnull-Z-p}$), against $\V{x-pre-erp-preonly-G}$ and $\V{xl-pre-erp-preonly-G}$ under the fixed scale, although the long-epoch paired difference does not exclude zero ($\V{xl-pre-erp-preonly-ZminusG}$, CI $\V{xl-pre-erp-preonly-ZminusG-ci}$). The conference reservoir window is at chance at the matched epoch ($\V{x-pre-res-conference-Z}$), and moved after onset it gains $\V{x-pre-postminusconf-Z}$ BA (CI $\V{x-pre-postminusconf-Z-ci}$), reaching $\V{x-pre-res-post-Z}$, a point estimate above that of the ERP-window encoder ($\V{x-clean-erp}$). \subsection{The Edge of Stability Across Reservoir Draws}
\label{sec:res-edge}
The rerun reproduces the conference transition of draw $42$ on SHAPE drives ($\rho^\ast=\V{se-rhostar-42}$). Across ten draws, $\rho^\ast=\V{se-rhostar-mean}\pm\V{se-rhostar-sd}$ on SHAPE drives (range $\V{se-rhostar-min}$ to $\V{se-rhostar-max}$, Table~\ref{tab:edge}) and $\V{e-rhostar-mean}\pm\V{e-rhostar-sd}$ on the external drives (range $\V{e-rhostar-min}$ to $\V{e-rhostar-max}$), and the draw-$42$ curves of the two cohorts agree within $\V{e-damage-maxdiff-42}$ at every grid point from $\rho=0$ to $2$. The transition is thus set by the reservoir and the statistics of its z-scored drive rather than by the cohort. Every draw on both cohorts places it below the echo-state heuristic $\rho=1$, and the conference draw lies near the top of the range. The unit moves the transition more than the draw does. On SHAPE drives, thresholds $0.25$, $0.5$, and $1.0$ place it at $\V{se-rhostar-theta0.25}$, $\V{se-rhostar-theta0.5}$, and $\V{se-rhostar-theta1.0}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:edge}c, where at $\vartheta=1.0$ the damage has not saturated by $\rho=2$, so this value is a lower bound), and from leak $0.02$ to $0.2$ it rises from $\V{se-rhostar-beta0.02}$ to $\V{se-rhostar-beta0.2}$, with the same pattern on the external drives. The heuristic $\rho=1$ therefore marks no transition for this spiking unit: depending on the threshold, the measured edge lies well below it or above it.

Clean accuracy does not follow the transition sharply (Fig.~\ref{fig:edge}b, Table~\ref{tab:edge}). With the conference window, mean clean BA over ten draws is $\V{se-ba-0.0-mean}$ without recurrence, $\V{se-ba-0.3-mean}$, $\V{se-ba-0.6-mean}$, and $\V{se-ba-0.9-mean}$ at $\rho=0.3$, $0.6$, and $0.9$, and $\V{se-ba-1.2-mean}$ and $\V{se-ba-1.5-mean}$ in the irregular regime, and the most accurate $\rho$ varies by draw. Removing the recurrence costs $\V{se-dm-0.0minus09-clean-abs}$ BA on average (CI $\V{se-dm-0.0minus09-clean-ci}$), and $\rho=0$ falls below $\rho=0.9$ in $\V{se-n-rho0-below-clean}$ of ten draws, so the pre-specified rule (nine of ten, with the interval of the draw mean excluding zero) is not met. The conference claim that the recurrent dynamics contribute, which rested on draw $42$ ($\V{s-rho0-minus-09}$, CI $\V{s-rho0-minus-09-ci}$), is therefore replaced by a broad optimum between $\rho=0.3$ and $0.9$. The window from onset gives the same average cost ($\V{seo-dm-0.0minus09-clean-abs}$, CI $\V{seo-dm-0.0minus09-clean-ci}$), now with an interval that excludes zero, but with $\rho=0$ below $\rho=0.9$ in only $\V{seo-n-rho0-below-clean}$ draws, so the rule is again not met. With the post window, mean clean BA is highest at $\rho=0.6$ ($\V{sep-ba-0.6-mean}$), removing the recurrence costs $\V{sep-dm-0.0minus09-clean-abs}$ on average (CI $\V{sep-dm-0.0minus09-clean-ci}$), and $\rho=0$ falls below $\rho=0.9$ in $\V{sep-n-rho0-below-clean}$ draws, so the rule is met on none of the three windows. The irregular regime costs accuracy with the conference window ($\rho=1.2$ minus $0.9$: $\V{se-dm-1.2minus09-clean}$, CI $\V{se-dm-1.2minus09-clean-ci}$, and $\rho=1.5$ minus $0.9$: $\V{se-dm-1.5minus09-clean}$, CI $\V{se-dm-1.5minus09-clean-ci}$) and the post window ($\rho=1.5$ minus $0.9$: $\V{sep-dm-1.5minus09-clean}$, CI $\V{sep-dm-1.5minus09-clean-ci}$) but not with the onset window ($\V{seo-dm-1.5minus09-clean}$, CI $\V{seo-dm-1.5minus09-clean-ci}$). On the external long epoch, five draws give a similarly flat profile, highest on average at $\rho=1.2$ (mean clean BA $\V{e-ba-0.0-mean}$ at $\rho=0$, $\V{e-ba-0.9-mean}$ at $0.9$, $\V{e-ba-1.2-mean}$ at $1.2$), and no single draw separates $\rho=0$ or $1.5$ from $0.9$ (Table~\ref{tab:edge-x}).

Electrode removal separates the regimes far more sharply than clean accuracy, once the window contains the evoked signal. With the onset window and $30\%$ of the electrodes silent, mean BA over ten draws is $\V{seo-ba30-0.0-mean}$ to $\V{seo-ba30-0.6-mean}$ for $\rho\le0.6$, a loss of at most $\V{seo-loss30-low-max}$, but $\V{seo-ba30-0.9-mean}$ to $\V{seo-ba30-1.5-mean}$ for $\rho\ge0.9$, losses of $\V{seo-loss30-high-min}$ to $\V{seo-loss30-high-max}$. Under removal the feedforward layer beats the conference operating point by $\V{seo-dm-0.0minus09-signal30}$ BA (draw mean, CI $\V{seo-dm-0.0minus09-signal30-ci}$). The post window gives the same split at larger size (losses at most $\V{sep-loss30-low-max}$ for $\rho\le0.6$ and $\V{sep-loss30-high-min}$ to $\V{sep-loss30-high-max}$ for $\rho\ge0.9$, and $\V{sep-dm-0.0minus09-signal30}$ in favor of the feedforward layer, CI $\V{sep-dm-0.0minus09-signal30-ci}$). The external long epoch shows the same split (losses at most $\V{e-loss30-low-max}$ for $\rho\le0.6$ and $\V{e-loss30-high-min}$ to $\V{e-loss30-high-max}$ for $\rho\ge0.9$, five draws). A silent electrode is a fixed point of every reservoir, so its block is zero spikes whatever $\rho$ is. Once activity rises past the transition, that silence lies far from the reservoir's typical state. For draw $42$ with the onset window, the silent-electrode block ($-P\bar b$ after PCA) sits on average $\V{seo-z0-low-min}$ to $\V{seo-z0-low-max}$ standard deviations from the training mean for $\rho\le0.6$ but $\V{seo-z0-high-min}$ to $\V{seo-z0-high-max}$ for $\rho\ge0.9$, as the mean firing rate over draws rises from $\V{seo-rate-0.6}$ spikes per unit and step at $\rho=0.6$ to $\V{seo-rate-1.2}$ at $\rho=1.2$ ($\V{e-z0-low-min}$ to $\V{e-z0-low-max}$ against $\V{e-z0-high-min}$ to $\V{e-z0-high-max}$ on the external long epoch). Past the measured edge of stability, nothing, in the literal sense of a silent electrode, lies far from anything the readout has seen. With the conference window, whose drive is mostly the near-constant pre-stimulus offset, the silent block stays at most about $\V{se-z0-all-max}$ standard deviations from the training mean at every $\rho$ and removal costs at most $\V{se-loss30-max}$ BA, which is why the conference sweep showed no such separation. The order parameter itself is measured with the conference definition, on drives that within its window are mostly that offset, so $\rho^\ast$ marks the transition for the onset-window code only approximately.

\subsection{Signal-Level Perturbations, Physical Repair, and Recorded Noise}
\label{sec:res-signal}
At the signal level every encoder sees the same corrupted test signal (Tables~\ref{tab:signal-shape} and~\ref{tab:signal-ext}, Fig.~\ref{fig:signal}). On SHAPE (conference results), ERP-window features led the reservoir in every condition, the reservoir held its accuracy through $30\%$ electrode removal but fell to $\V{s-sig-remove0.5-res}$ at $50\%$, $5$~dB noise cost band-power $\V{s-loss-amp5dB-band-abs}$ BA, and $\pm50$~ms jitter moved no fixed encoder.

\emph{The chance floor in practice.} At the external conference-matched epoch, where no fixed encoder is far from chance, perturbations move BA in both directions: the reservoir scores $\V{x-sig-clean-res}$ clean and $\V{x-sig-amp5dB-res}$ under $5$~dB noise, a raw retention of $\V{x-rawret-amp5dB-res}$. A ratio above one here measures nothing about stability. It is the raw-retention artifact of the conference paper appearing without being sought. We therefore read the external signal-level results on the long epoch.

\emph{Electrode removal and spline repair.} On the long epoch, removing electrodes as zero traces costs the reservoir $\V{xl-loss-remove0.3-res-abs}$ BA at $30\%$ (CI $\V{xl-loss-remove0.3-res-ci}$) and $\V{xl-loss-remove0.5-res-abs}$ at $50\%$ ($\V{xl-loss-remove0.5-res-ci}$), and band-power $\V{xl-loss-remove0.5-band-abs}$ at $50\%$, while ERP-window features lose $\V{xl-loss-remove0.5-erp-abs}$ at $50\%$. ERP-window therefore leads the reservoir by $\V{xl-sig-remove0.5-erpres}$ (CI $\V{xl-sig-remove0.5-erpres-ci}$) with half the electrodes removed, a larger lead than on clean data. Repairing the same electrodes by spherical-spline interpolation (correlation $\V{xl-splinecorr-50}$ with the true trace at $50\%$) removes that lead: with the repaired signal no fixed encoder loses more than $\V{xl-spline50-maxloss}$ BA at $50\%$, the reservoir gains $\V{xl-splminuszero50-res}$ over the zero trace (CI $\V{xl-splminuszero50-res-ci}$), band-power gains $\V{xl-splminuszero50-band}$ ($\V{xl-splminuszero50-band-ci}$), and the ERP-window lead falls to $\V{xl-sig-spline0.5-erpres}$ ($\V{xl-sig-spline0.5-erpres-ci}$). The signal-level ranking under electrode loss was set by representing the absent electrode as a zero trace. A zero trace is a silent drive, and for the reservoir silence lies far from its typical state (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-edge}).

\emph{Noise and jitter.} The SHAPE pattern replicates: $5$~dB noise costs band-power $\V{xl-loss-amp5dB-band-abs}$ BA (CI $\V{xl-loss-amp5dB-band-ci}$, against $\V{s-loss-amp5dB-band-abs}$ on SHAPE) and leaves ERP-window and the reservoir within noise, and $\pm50$~ms jitter moves no fixed encoder.

\emph{Recorded observation noise.} Re-averaging each test observation from fewer of its own trials lowers ERP-window BA from $\V{xl-sig-clean-erp}$ to $\V{xl-sig-trials1-erp}$ at one trial (loss $\V{xl-loss-trials1-erp-abs}$, CI $\V{xl-loss-trials1-erp-ci}$), whereas the reservoir is unchanged at one trial ($\V{xl-sig-trials1-res}$, loss $\V{xl-loss-trials1-res-abs}$, CI $\V{xl-loss-trials1-res-ci}$) and band-power loses $\V{xl-loss-trials1-band-abs}$ ($\V{xl-loss-trials1-band-ci}$) (Fig.~\ref{fig:signal}a). The paired ERP-window minus reservoir difference moves from $\V{xl-sig-clean-erpres}$ on clean data to $\V{xl-sig-trials1-erpres}$ at one trial (CI $\V{xl-sig-trials1-erpres-ci}$), so the encoders are not separable on single trials. Training used the full averages throughout, so these numbers measure how each fixed representation transfers from trial averages to noisier observations.

\emph{Training effect.} EEGNet, trained end to end on the signal, is the most accurate encoder at the external cohort's conference-matched epoch, as on SHAPE. There, where every fixed encoder is near chance, it reaches $\V{x-clean-eegnet}$ (CI $\V{x-clean-eegnet-ci}$) without and $\V{x-clean-aug}$ with augmentation, $\V{x-eegnetminuserp-clean}$ above ERP-window (CI $\V{x-eegnetminuserp-clean-ci}$), and its paired lead over ERP-window has a lower bound at or above zero in every condition (Table~\ref{tab:signal-x}). The SHAPE training effect does not replicate in size. On SHAPE, channel-dropout and noise augmentation cut EEGNet's loss to electrode removal by more than half ($\V{s-loss-remove0.5-eegnet-abs}$ to $\V{s-loss-remove0.5-aug-abs}$ BA at $50\%$). \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\emph{Absence is a coordinate.} The conference result was that zero-fill halves an encoder difference. The rerun shows what that rested on and what generalizes. What generalizes is the input-level geometry and its consequence for the dropout score. On both cohorts each encoder's dropped block sits at nearly the same standardized distance from its training mean, the fill choice rewrites labels in the order of that distance, and moving the fill along the standardized axis changes the dropout score while clean predictions stay fixed up to solver tolerance, by up to $\V{sj-o30-k0minuskm30-erp-abs}$ BA for SHAPE's ERP-window features. What does not generalize is any single zero-versus-mean contrast. Its size depends on where the native zero falls relative to the decision boundaries, which changed with the ERP windows (the conference halving vanished when they were measured from onset), and on the above-chance margin, which limits what a fill can cost (the external cohort). A dropout comparison that zero-fills is therefore a comparison of coordinate systems as much as of representations. This agrees with the observations that channel-dropout fragility of EEG foundation models depends on whether dropped channels are removed or zero-padded~\cite{sirca2026beyond} and that how a channel goes missing or out of place changes measured tolerance~\cite{wang2026evaluation}, and Proposition~1 with the origin sweep gives the mechanism and its size for standardized features. The reservoir's indifference to the fill belongs to its PCA coordinates: its raw spike counts are about as far from centered as band-power, and moved far from its own mean its code degrades too.

\emph{The empty baseline is not empty after normalization.} Per-epoch z-scoring is a common default, and Proposition~2 shows that it writes $-\tfrac{|Q|}{T}m_Q/s$ into every pre-stimulus sample. On SHAPE that is enough for the $200$~ms recorded before the picture to classify affect at $\V{sj-pre-erp-preonly-Z}$ BA, and for the conference reservoir, whose window lies mostly before onset, to reach its conference accuracy from the offset: under a fixed scale it falls to chance, and its code predicts the post-stimulus mean almost perfectly. The mechanism resembles the way high-pass filters move late ERP effects to earlier latencies~\cite{acunzo2012systematic,tanner2015inappropriate,vanrullen2011four}, and, because the normalization uses the whole epoch, it is a form of leakage from the post-stimulus signal into the baseline~\cite{kapoor2023leakage}. It moves information within a subject's own epoch, not across subjects, so it does not inflate accuracy the way cross-subject leakage does, but it misattributes it: a window read as early dynamics measures late amplitude. The conference accuracy of the reservoir therefore describes how it encodes a normalization offset, not evoked dynamics, and the recurrence effect measured through the same window cannot separate the two (the onset window gives the same draw-mean cost, Sec.~\ref{sec:res-edge}). Placed after onset, the same reservoir measures the evoked signal on both cohorts, over the whole post-onset interval with accuracy not distinguishable from the ERP-window encoder on SHAPE, and its temporal bins then carry information that the conference window's did not. A window close to onset can still read part of the offset under z-scoring, so the fixed-scale check belongs with any window choice. Two practical checks follow: decode from the pre-stimulus interval alone, and repeat the analysis with a fixed scale.

\emph{The edge of stability.} The conference reported a transition near $\rho^\ast\approx0.82$ for one reservoir draw. Ten draws place it at $\V{se-rhostar-mean}$ on SHAPE drives and $\V{e-rhostar-mean}$ on the external drives, always below $\rho=1$, but the firing threshold moves it from $\V{se-rhostar-theta0.25}$ to at least $\V{se-rhostar-theta1.0}$. For a thresholded spiking reservoir the edge is therefore a measured property of the unit and its drive rather than a value fixed by the spectral radius, in line with edge-of-chaos analyses of other reservoirs~\cite{bertschinger2004realtime,legenstein2007edge,boedecker2012information} and with the result that $\rho<1$ is neither necessary nor sufficient for the echo-state property~\cite{yildiz2012revisiting}. Clean accuracy was broad around the transition on both cohorts, and the recurrence effect that the conference measured on one draw did not meet the decision rule fixed for ten. The operating point mattered most under electrode removal. With the onset window, past the transition a silent electrode lies far from the reservoir's typical code and removal cost $\V{seo-loss30-high-min}$ to $\V{seo-loss30-high-max}$ BA on SHAPE, against at most $\V{seo-loss30-low-max}$ below it, and the post window and the external long epoch showed the same split. An operating point chosen near the edge for clean accuracy is thus the one least tolerant of absent inputs, and measuring both is cheap.

\emph{Training and physical repair.} Two conference conclusions about electrode loss change with the second cohort. First, augmentation reduced EEGNet's loss to electrode removal on the external cohort by at most $\V{rc-lossred-max}$ BA across four recipes on one partition, and not at $50\%$ removal over five partitions with the default recipe. The conference finding that the training procedure sets this architecture's accuracy under electrode loss therefore holds for SHAPE, where augmentation halved the loss, and not as a general property of the architecture. On the external cohort augmentation acted mostly as a general gain in accuracy. Second, physical repair changed the ranking that the zero trace produced. With spline-repaired electrodes no fixed encoder lost more than $\V{xl-spline50-maxloss}$ BA at $50\%$ removal on the long epoch, EEGNet recovered most of its removal loss, and the ERP-window lead over the reservoir disappeared, although at the conference-matched epoch, where the encoders are near chance, the lead persisted after repair. A zero trace is itself a fill, the signal-level counterpart of zero-fill, and the same advice applies: state it, and compare it with a physically motivated repair~\cite{perrin1989spherical} before ranking representations by their tolerance of missing electrodes.

\emph{A reporting standard.} The results support five reporting items for EEG representation studies under perturbation. First, absolute accuracy with subject-level uncertainty and an empirical permutation floor~\cite{combrisson2015exceeding,varoquaux2018cross}, never retention ratios alone. Second, the fill rule, varied along the standardized axis rather than as one contrast, together with the mean $|\mu|/\sigma$ of the features and the above-chance margin, which together limit how much the rule can matter. Third, the normalization, whether any window used for decoding precedes onset, and the accuracy of the pre-stimulus interval alone. Fourth, trained-model results with and without augmentation~\cite{rommel2022augment}. Fifth, for reservoirs, the measured transition $\rho^\ast$ and more than one weight draw.

\emph{Limitations.} The SHAPE export documents neither its channel order nor its single trials, so spline repair and recorded trial-count noise were measured on the external cohort only, whose above-chance margin is about a third of SHAPE's. The windows measured from onset were fixed in advance, but they are two of many possible windows, no other rerun windows were analyzed, and they were analyzed under z-scoring, which near onset can still carry part of the offset. The external analysis used one pre-specified long epoch beyond the conference-matched one. The reservoir's draws, thresholds, and leaks were varied only around the conference design, EEGNet is the only trained architecture and was not rerun on SHAPE, although it reads the z-scored pre-stimulus interval, so how much of its accuracy that interval carries is untested, and DEAP was not reanalyzed. The fixed per-channel scale is pooled over all observations without labels, which is transductive with respect to subject inclusion. The external cohort's authors interpolated some bad channels before release, which may favor spline repair there. Clinical labels, graph topology, and structure--function coupling are outside the scope of this paper.

\section{Conclusion}
What an evaluation lets stand for nothing is part of the measurement. A zero-filled electrode sits at the coordinate origin, so its effect on dropout accuracy is set by where that origin lies relative to the data and the decision boundaries, and by how much the encoder has above chance. Per-epoch normalization writes a scaled copy of the post-stimulus mean into the baseline, and on SHAPE that was enough for the empty interval before onset to classify affect and for the conference reservoir to score through it. The edge of stability of a fixed spiking reservoir is a measured quantity that moves with the firing threshold more than with the weight draw, so the echo-state heuristic $\rho=1$ marks no transition for this unit, and past the transition silence lies far from the reservoir's typical state. Placed after onset, the reservoir measures the evoked signal, over the whole post-onset interval with accuracy not distinguishable from that of the ERP-window encoder on SHAPE, and its near-centered code and controllable dynamics make each of these effects visible.
