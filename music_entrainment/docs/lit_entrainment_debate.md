# Neural entrainment vs. superposition of evoked responses to rhythmic auditory input (2011–2026): state of the art, diagnostic tests, and novelty assessment

Prepared 2026-11-08. Sources were located with Consensus, PubMed (metadata records; DOIs given inline per PubMed terms), alphaXiv, and web search/fetch of open-access full texts (PMC). Items not confirmed by a tool are marked **[unverified]**. Numbers quoted from papers were read from the full text where noted (Damsma 2025, Doelling 2019, Novembre & Iannetti 2018, Rajendran & Schnupp 2019, Doelling & Assaneo 2021, Zoefel et al. 2018, Keitel et al. 2014, Lenc et al. 2018 reply); otherwise from abstracts.

Corrections to the citation list in the brief (all verified via PubMed/web):
- Lenc, Keller, Varlet & Nozaradan 2018 is **PNAS** 115:8221 (not Sci Adv). The 2019 exchange with Rajendran & Schnupp is also in PNAS (not Frontiers). The "2023 periodicity-tagging critique" by Lenc et al. appears to be the autocorrelation/self-similarity methods paper, published in **Eur J Neurosci 2025** (bioRxiv earlier).
- Zalta, Large, Schön & Morillon 2024 is **Science Advances** (not Nat Commun). Zalta, Petkoski & Morillon 2020 is the Nat Commun paper (behavioral, "Natural rhythms of periodic temporal attention").
- Doelling et al. 2019 used a **Wilson–Cowan** E/I oscillator (not Stuart–Landau); Doelling & Assaneo 2021 used Stuart–Landau for their cautionary simulations.
- van Bree et al. "Sustained neural rhythms..." is PLoS Biol **2021** (online 2020).
- Kaneshiro et al. 2020 (NeuroImage) used N=48 and intact/measure-shuffled/reversed/phase-scrambled songs; I could not confirm that it is the NMED-T dataset (NMED-T is Losorelli et al., ISMIR 2017, N=20, 10 songs, 55–150 bpm per a secondary source).
- Rajendran, Harper & Schnupp 2020 is R Soc Open Sci (rat A1 firing rates; STRF model), not a dynamical-systems theory paper.

### 1.3 The oscillator-model camp and the phase-concentration test

| Paper | Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Doelling, Assaneo, Bevilacqua, Pesaran, Poeppel 2019, PNAS 116:10113 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1816414116) | Evoked model (fixed kernel convolved with envelope) predicts phase lag growing with note rate (Δϕ=2πΔt/λ, PCM≈0.17–0.25); Wilson–Cowan oscillator (τ=66 ms, ~4 Hz, κ=1.5) predicts phase concentration (PCM≈0.58); MEG at 1–8 notes/s is closer to the oscillator (AIC Δ 2.9–27.6), more so for smooth attacks. ## 2. Diagnostic tests and their status

| Test | Logic | Who used it | Who criticised it / status | Does Damsma 2025 (or others) show it is non-diagnostic? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Phase lag vs rate: constant phase (oscillator) vs constant latency (evoked) → phase concentration (PCM) | Fixed kernel ⇒ Δϕ = 2πΔt/λ grows with rate; oscillator keeps phase within its synchrony range | Doelling et al. 2019 (MEG, 1–8 nps; data closer to oscillator); Damsma et al. 2025 (EEG, 5 tempi; data closer to evoked, in between); ASSR field uses slope as "apparent latency" (Wang 2020) | Doelling 2019 themselves: kernels exist "that maintain a constant phase lag within some frequency range" and an adaptive evoked model "may also be described as an oscillation"; Doelling & Assaneo 2021: LIF is both; PCM depends on the estimated kernel (Damsma: kernel "increases the risk of capturing ongoing oscillatory activity"). Any dispersive linear filter (group delay scaling with period) yields constant phase in-band. | **Yes, partially**: Damsma 2025 obtained PCM between the two models and concluded for mixed contributions; the two published applications reach opposite verdicts. As a binary diagnostic it is non-diagnostic; as a parametric model comparison (with a kernel family that includes dispersive/adaptive kernels) it remains informative. |

## 3. Has anyone driven a nonlinear fixed dynamical system with EEG/MEG to test TRF-residual structure, or used nonlinear-TRF tests?

- **Nonlinear encoding vs linear STRF/TRF (closest in spirit)**: Keshishian et al. 2020 (eLife) and Mischler et al. 2022 (NeuroImage) fit DNNs to iEEG and quantify the gain over linear STRFs, interpreting the networks as stimulus-dependent linear filters. Bellier et al. 2023 used nonlinear decoders for music iEEG. Puffay et al. 2023 review 29 DNN EEG–speech studies and list the methodological failure modes (biased CV, leakage, model size vs data). Drennan & Lalor 2019 is the simplest "nonlinear evoked" TRF (amplitude-binned). None of these frame the gain as evidence for entrainment.
- **TRF-recovered resonance**: Zhigalov, Duecker & Jensen 2021 estimate a TRF to broadband flicker and find subject-specific gamma and alpha echoes—i.e., resonant dynamics show up *inside* a linear kernel, which is why "residual after TRF" is a poor proxy for "oscillatory".
- **Evoked-twin constructions**: Capilla 2011 (shift-and-add ERPs), Doelling 2019 (tone-evoked kernel convolved with envelope), Damsma 2025 (cross-session mTRF kernel on onsets). These compare summary statistics of twin vs data; none analyse the residual with a phase-randomised surrogate.
- **Reservoirs/SNNs fed with EEG**: HeteroRC (Lu et al. 2026, bioRxiv) is the closest: a heterogeneous reservoir on raw EEG that decodes non-phase-locked features that linear decoders miss. Ioannides et al. 2021 trained spiking RNNs to reproduce EEG. Kotz et al. 2026 (bioRxiv) use SNNs as *models of* entrainment (stimulus in, spikes out), not as readouts of EEG. Many SNN/LSM papers classify EEG (attention, emotion, motor) but test no mechanism.
- **Volterra/second-order**: Only classical auditory-system Volterra/Wiener work (Eggermont 1993; Shi & Hecox 1991 m-pulse BAER) and the ASSR "apparent latency from phase coherence" method (Wang et al. 2020) surfaced; no Volterra-residual test for beat entrainment in EEG was found.

Bottom line: nobody appears to have published the specific "fit TRF → phase-randomise residual → nonlinear readout → compare" pipeline for beat EEG. That gap exists partly because the field already knows the test cannot separate oscillators from nonlinear evoked responses (Section 2b).

## 4. What is the open, publishable question (2025–2026)?

Consensus across camps (Doelling & Assaneo 2021; Haegens & Zion Golumbic 2018; Zoefel 2018; Duecker 2024; Damsma 2025): stop asking "oscillation or not" and compare explicit, falsifiable models on the same data with an evoked model as baseline. Within that frame the live questions are:

1. **Which evoked model is sufficient?** Damsma 2025 shows a *fixed-kernel* evoked model mimics tempo-selective enhancement but under-predicts phase concentration (0.23 vs 0.41). The open question is whether an evoked model with adaptation/refractoriness/IOI-dependent amplitude and amplitude-dependent latency (Rajendran 2017 adaptation; Drennan & Lalor 2019) closes that gap, or whether a term with phase memory (oscillator/GFNN/Bayesian phase inference) is required. Nobody has fit both families to naturalistic music EEG.
2. **Context dependence and prediction**: hysteresis (Lenc 2020), silent-beat/omission responses (Tal 2017; Di Liberto 2021; Bouwer 2023), and tempo-flexible phase are the phenomena a fixed-kernel model cannot produce. A contribution shows which of these survive a strong adaptive-evoked baseline.
3. **Measurement validity**: phase-sensitive beat-locking metrics (Rajendran & Schnupp 2019; Lenc 2025 autocorrelation), stimulus-signal controls (López-Madrona 2026), and replication at scale (Nave 2025, N=152 null). A new metric must be validated on simulated oscillator vs evoked twins before use on data.
4. **Naturalistic music specifically**: tracking is weaker than for speech (Zuk 2021), spectral flux beats envelope (Weineck 2022), and beat-frequency coherence does not explain time-domain ISC (Kaneshiro 2020). Whether any beat-locked component in real music EEG exceeds a well-specified linear+adaptive evoked model is open.

What reviewers at J Neurosci / NeuroImage / PLoS Comp Biol / eLife / JoCN would call a contribution vs a rehash:

- Contribution: explicit model comparison (oscillator family vs rich evoked family) with cross-validated predictive accuracy on naturalistic music; metrics validated on synthetic twins; stimulus-level controls; behavioural anchoring (tapping); preregistration or multi-dataset replication; a demonstrated result that a *specific* oscillator property (eigenfrequency, intermittency, tempo-flexible phase memory) is required.
- Rehash: another frequency-tagging or PCM result interpreted as "entrainment"; "beyond linear" claims resting on a single ridge-regression TRF; tempo-selective enhancement; offset-persistence without a filter-ringing control; a new nonlinear readout without showing it beats trivial nonlinear statistics (ITPC/PLV of the residual at the beat frequency).

## 5. Novelty assessment for the proposed study

Proposal: drive a fixed 256-neuron LIF reservoir (ARSPI-Net) with (a) real NMED-T EEG and (b) an "evoked twin" = cross-validated forward mTRF prediction + phase-randomised residual; compare the beat-phase modulation index of reservoir spiking and the phase-lag-vs-tempo slope across 10 songs/tempi; claim "beat-locked structure beyond linear superposition" if real > twin.

### 5.1 What is genuinely new (not found in the literature)
- A **surrogate that preserves the exact cross-validated linear prediction while destroying all stimulus-locked structure in the residual** (phase randomisation of the residual), applied to naturalistic music EEG. Capilla 2011, Doelling 2019 and Damsma 2025 built evoked twins but compared summary statistics, not residuals, and none used phase-randomised residual surrogates.
- Using a **fixed spiking reservoir as a nonlinear readout of EEG** in an entrainment question. Only HeteroRC (2026 preprint, decoding) is close.
- Applying any of this to **NMED-T** (10 songs, 55–150 bpm, tapping available). Weineck 2022 and Kaneshiro 2020 did TRF/RCA on naturalistic music but not a residual test.

### 5.2 What is already done
- Evoked-twin baselines by kernel convolution: Capilla 2011 (visual), Doelling 2019 (MEG music), Damsma 2025 (EEG rhythms, mTRF kernel). The concept is established; your variant differs only in the surrogate.
- Phase-lag-vs-tempo / phase concentration across tempi: Doelling 2019 (6 rates, factor 8 range) and Damsma 2025 (5 tempi, factor 4). Ten songs spanning 55–150 bpm give a factor <3 range with song-specific confounds (instrumentation, spectral flux, familiarity, lyrics), i.e., a weaker version of a test that has already produced opposite verdicts in two labs.
- Tempo-dependence of linear beat tracking in naturalistic music: Weineck 2022 (strongest at 1–2 Hz; spectral flux > envelope).
- Beat-related frequency-domain structure in naturalistic music with RCA: Kaneshiro 2020.
- Nonlinear-vs-linear encoding gains: Keshishian 2020, Mischler 2022, Bellier 2023 (iEEG), with the methodological warnings of Puffay 2023.

### 5.3 What is known or likely to be non-diagnostic (be blunt)
1. **"Beyond linear superposition" ≠ "not evoked".** Doelling & Assaneo 2021: "a leaky integrate-and-fire model is both an oscillator and a model of evoked responses." Rajendran 2017/2020 show adaptation and STRFs (nonlinear evoked) produce beat emphasis. Residual beat-locking after a linear TRF is evidence against *linear* superposition only, and the field moved past linear superposition as the null after Tal 2017. If the manuscript claims entrainment, reviewers will reject; if it claims "nonlinear stimulus-locked structure", the result must be compared to a nonlinear evoked baseline (amplitude-binned/adaptive TRF; Damsma's IOI/habituation kernel), not to a ridge TRF.
2. **The residual of a single ridge mTRF is not a clean null.** Residual stimulus-locked variance is expected from feature misspecification (envelope vs spectral flux vs onsets vs beat impulses), lag truncation, shrinkage, latency jitter, non-stationarity across a 4–5 min song, and attention drift. With one presentation per song per subject (NMED-T), kernel estimates are noisy and the residual will contain beat-locked linear leakage. "Real > twin" is therefore nearly guaranteed and uninformative unless the linear family is maximised (multi-feature, per-channel, nested CV with λ and lag range selected out-of-sample, and reported effect sizes vs a *shifted-residual* surrogate as well as a phase-randomised one).
3. **The reservoir adds no diagnostic power by itself.** The beat-phase modulation index of reservoir spikes is a nonlinear functional of the input EEG; a real > twin difference is a statement about the input's residual, not about the reservoir. Reviewers will ask why ITPC/PLV or a bispectrum of the residual at the beat frequency does not suffice, and will require that the reservoir readout be benchmarked against those trivial statistics and against a shuffled-weights reservoir. A reservoir tuned to the "edge of stability" can also carry slow intrinsic modes; show input-free and surrogate-input runs to rule out self-generated beat-phase concentration.
4. **Phase-lag-vs-tempo slope is not a discriminator** (Section 2a): Doelling 2019 concede constant-phase linear kernels exist; Damsma 2025 found the data between models. Over 0.9–2.5 Hz with heterogeneous songs, the slope estimate will be dominated by song differences. At most, report it descriptively; do not build a claim on it.
5. **Tempo-selective enhancement is non-diagnostic** (Damsma 2025). Any "beat-locking peaks at mid tempi" result will be read as the preferred-rate evoked effect.
6. **Stimulus controls are mandatory** (López-Madrona 2026): beat-locked residual structure can come from stimulus features absent from the TRF (vocals, percussion transients, harmonic rhythm). Include a stimulus-only analysis (reservoir driven by the audio features) and a rich feature set.
7. **Statistical power**: NMED-T has N=20 and 10 songs; Nave 2025 shows frequency-tagged beat effects are small (0.03 µV) at N=152. Pre-specify the metric, validate it on synthetic oscillator and evoked twins with NMED-T noise levels, and report Bayes factors or equivalence tests.

### 5.4 How to make it publishable
- Reframe: "Does naturalistic-music EEG contain beat-locked structure not captured by a maximal linear encoding model, and is that structure better explained by an adaptive-evoked or an oscillator model?" Fit both families to the residual (or to the data) and compare out-of-sample; the reservoir becomes one of several nonlinear readouts whose sensitivity is characterised on simulated twins.
- Validate the pipeline on Damsma 2025's public design logic (or simulated GFNN vs adaptive-evoked data) before NMED-T.
- Anchor to behaviour: relate residual beat-locking to NMED-T tapping consistency per song/subject (Nozaradan 2016; Weineck 2022 precedent).
- Suitable venues for the reframed, model-comparison version: NeuroImage, PLoS Comput Biol, eNeuro, J Cogn Neurosci. J Neurosci/eLife would need a decisive mechanistic result (e.g., tempo-flexible phase memory or silent-beat responses surviving the adaptive-evoked baseline). As currently framed ("beat-locked structure beyond linear superposition if real > twin"), expect reviewers to call it a known-non-diagnostic rehash of Doelling 2019/Damsma 2025 with a weaker tempo range and an unjustified nonlinear readout.
