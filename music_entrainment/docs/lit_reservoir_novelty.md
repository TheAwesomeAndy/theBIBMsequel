# Novelty check: fixed LIF reservoir as a "measurement instrument" for beat-phase structure in music EEG

Date: 2026-11-08. Tools used: Consensus, alphaXiv, PubMed, WebSearch/WebFetch. Items marked **[unverified]** were not confirmed by any tool call in this session; everything else was located by at least one tool (bibliographic record and abstract-level claim). Full texts were not read unless stated, so one-sentence claims are abstract-level.

Scope of the check: (1) RC/LSM/ESN/SNN on EEG/MEG for rhythm, SSVEP/ASSR, music, phase-locking; (2) fixed nonlinear system as detector of nonlinear structure vs. surrogates / nonlinear TRFs; (3) beat-phase binning / modulation index of neural activity during music; (4) neural-resonance (GrFNN) models vs. EEG; (5) "measurement operator / observer" framings of fixed reservoirs.

## (B) Novelty verdict

**Specific combination queried:** fixed, untrained 256-neuron LIF reservoir driven by z-scored continuous naturalistic-music EEG → binned spike counts → (i) beat-phase modulation index of population spiking, with (ii) comparison against a linear-TRF-based "evoked twin" (EEG predicted from stimulus features, or TRF-residual-matched surrogate) to isolate structure beyond the linear evoked response.

**Verdict: no located prior work implements this combination.** Decomposing it:

1. *Fixed spiking reservoir on music EEG.* Not found. Reservoirs on EEG exist for classification (1.1, 1.10, 1.12, Fourati 2018), SSVEP detection (1.2), epileptic-state regression on synthetic LFP (1.9), and motor/attention decoding with interpretability (HeteroRC 1.11). None uses music or rhythm EEG.

2. *Beat-phase MI of reservoir population spiking.* Not found. Beat-phase-binned neural measures exist as frequency tagging (3.4–3.6, 3.10), beta power modulation (3.11), ECoG high-gamma autocorrelation (3.9), and PAC/MI in rhythm EEG (3.20), but always on the recorded signal, never on the output of a fixed nonlinear transform of it.

3. *Linear-TRF "evoked twin" as the null for a nonlinear detector.* The closest precedents are: stimulus-side surrogates in naturalistic-music EEG (Kaneshiro 2020, 2.12); oscillator-vs-evoked model discrimination (Doelling 2019, 4.6; Damsma 2025, 4.7); nonlinear-vs-linear decoder comparisons for speech (2.3–2.6); and MI-based "beyond linear" tracking (De Clercq 2022). No paper feeds a linear-model-predicted EEG and the real EEG through the same fixed nonlinear system and compares phase-locked outputs.

4. *Reservoir as detector rather than classifier.* Precedent exists in Ibáñez-Soria 2017/2019 (generalized synchronization; SSVEP non-stationarity) and in the reservoir-observer / KKL literature (5.1–5.5), and HeteroRC (2026) explicitly argues reservoirs reveal non-phase-locked EEG structure. The "measurement operator" framing therefore has a citable lineage but has not been applied to beat-phase structure in music EEG.

**Closest existing work (ranked):**
- Ibáñez-Soria et al. 2019 PLoS ONE (1.2): fixed ESN vs stationary linear method on an EEG steady-state response; interprets disagreement as non-stationary structure. Differences: non-spiking, visual SSVEP, classification-ITR endpoint, no phase-binned analysis, no model-matched surrogate.
- HeteroRC 2026 preprint (1.11): reservoir states expose non-phase-locked EEG structure beyond linear decoders, with interpretability. Differences: trained readout decoding of task labels, motor/attention tasks, no stimulus-locked phase analysis.
- Doelling et al. 2019 PNAS (4.6) and Damsma et al. 2025 Cereb Cortex (4.7): model-based discrimination of oscillatory vs evoked accounts of beat-frequency responses. Differences: parametric oscillator/evoked models fit to M/EEG, controlled stimuli, not a fixed reservoir, not naturalistic single-listen music.
- Tichko et al. 2022 Brain Sci (3.14) and Weineck et al. 2021 eLife (3.13): naturalistic-music scalp EEG with pulse-level phase-locking, the former using GrFNN-estimated frequencies. Differences: linear PLV/TRF metrics only.
- Yuan, Wiggins, Botteldooren 2025 (1.6) and Kawai/Fujii/Asada 2024 (1.5): oscillatory reservoirs for rhythm, but as perception/production models without EEG.

**What is not novel and should be cited as such:** the surrogate-data logic (Theiler 1992; Schreiber & Schmitz 1996), the MI estimator (Tort 2010), edge-of-chaos calibration (Verstraeten 2007; Boedecker 2012), the observer framing (Lu 2017; Bizyaeva 2026), the evoked-vs-oscillator debate (Doelling 2019; Damsma 2025; Breska & Deouell 2017; Rajendran & Schnupp 2019), and the "beyond linear TRF" motivation (Puffay 2023; De Clercq 2022; Drennan & Lalor 2019).

## (C) Risks reviewers would raise, with supporting literature

1. **"Why a reservoir instead of a direct nonlinearity test?"** Established alternatives need no random network: IAAFT surrogates with any discriminating statistic (Theiler 1992; Schreiber & Schmitz 1996); mutual-information tracking (De Clercq 2022); amplitude-binned mTRFs (Drennan & Lalor 2019); explicit nonlinearity detection in TRF pipelines (Wilroth 2024); CNN-TRFs with interpretable linearizations (Keshishian 2020). Reviewers will ask for at least one of these as a positive control and for a demonstration that the reservoir statistic adds detection power or interpretability over them. The Puffay et al. 2023 review documents how nonlinear EEG–stimulus models repeatedly fell to leakage and overfitting; a fixed reservoir avoids readout training but not seed/hyperparameter selection effects.

2. **Spectral/phase artefacts of rectification and binning.** Spike counts are rectified, thresholded, binned transforms. Zhou et al. (2016) show that periodic responses generate harmonics and may lack power at f; Rajendran & Schnupp (2019) argue frequency-tagging-type measures discard phase and temporal structure (Lenc et al. 2019 rebut); Keshavarzi (2026) shows MI is blind to 180° phase polarity; Hülsemann et al. (2019) and Jurkiewicz et al. (2020) document MI sensitivity to data length, non-sinusoidality and sharp edges. A reviewer will ask whether beat-phase MI of spike counts reflects beat-locked neural structure or the LIF threshold acting on any beat-locked linear envelope. The evoked-twin comparison is the right answer, but only if the twin is passed through the identical reservoir and identical binning and the MI null distribution is built from reservoir outputs of IAAFT/TRF-residual surrogates, not from raw-EEG surrogates.

3. **Entrainment vs evoked tracking is not decidable from beat-locked phase measures alone.** Damsma et al. (2025) show oscillator and evoked models both reproduce tempo-dependent beat-frequency enhancement in EEG; Breska & Deouell (2017) show delta phase concentration reflects temporal predictability rather than rhythmic entrainment; Doelling et al. (2019) needed a rate-manipulation design to separate the accounts; Rajendran et al. (2017) show subcortical adaptation alone produces on-beat emphasis. Any claim that reservoir-detected beat-phase structure indicates "nonlinear entrainment" rather than nonlinearly transformed evoked responses will be challenged unless the design manipulates tempo or includes missing-pulse / syncopated conditions (Tal 2017; Nozaradan 2012).

4. **Instrument calibration and reproducibility: randomness, regime sensitivity, and the "edge of stability."** Reservoir outputs depend on the random connectivity seed, spectral radius/gain, input scaling and leak; information processing changes sharply at the order–chaos boundary (Boedecker 2012; Verstraeten 2007) and there is a phase transition between linear and nonlinear processing regimes (Osaka 2025, arXiv:2505.13003), with optimal reservoir nonlinearity depending on data nonlinearity (LMU/DLR 2025, arXiv:2504.17503). Woo et al. (2023) and Freddi et al. (2025) tie LSM performance to criticality. A reviewer will demand: multiple seeds with reported dispersion of MI, a sweep across gain/leak showing the MI effect is not confined to a knife-edge regime, and a statement of what the instrument's "transfer characteristic" is (e.g., its response to pure sinusoids and to IAAFT surrogates at matched spectra). Shahi et al. 2022 and Viehweg et al. 2024/2025 document the "high uncertainty and many random initializations" cost of classical RC and propose deterministic alternatives (NG-RC, Gauthier 2021), which a reviewer may suggest as a more interpretable instrument.

5. **Validity and interpretability of z-scored EEG as current injection.** Direct current injection of a 1/f, non-stationary, artefact-prone scalp signal into LIF units makes firing rate a function of amplitude, offset and channel variance; the reservoir "sees" bandwidth- and amplitude-dependent features before any beat-related dynamics. HeteroRC (2026) addresses interpretability with a dedicated sensor/source attribution layer; Keshishian (2020) offers input-dependent linearization. Reviewers will ask for channel-dropout, amplitude-noise and temporal-jitter perturbation curves (as in the BIBM protocol), per-channel contribution maps, and a check that beat-phase MI does not simply track the EEG amplitude envelope or eye/motion artefacts time-locked to the beat (Kaneshiro-style phase-scrambled stimulus controls help here).

6. *(Minor)* **Spiking vs non-spiking reservoir.** Liu et al. (2026, *AI*) and the broader LSM literature show spiking reservoirs are mainly motivated by hardware efficiency; absent an energy/latency claim (excluded from this paper's scope), a reviewer may ask why not an ESN or NG-RC, which Ibáñez-Soria 2019 already applied to EEG steady-state responses. The defensible answer is the thresholded, event-based transform itself being the measurement of interest, which must then be stated and tested explicitly.
